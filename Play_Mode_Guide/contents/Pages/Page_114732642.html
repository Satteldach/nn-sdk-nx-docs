3 Three Play Modes

This section introduces the following three play modes supported by the Switch.


  • TV Mode
  • Tabletop Mode
  • Handheld Mode
  • Play Modes


Note

Other development documentation introduced two different modes: console and handheld. These modes are defined to represent system states from either the perspective of the Switch or the application.

The three play modes defined in this section are types of play from the user perspective.

The correspondence between modes and types of play is as follows.


Play Mode System Operating Mode
TV Mode Console Mode
Tabletop Mode Handheld Mode
Handheld Mode

To prevent confusion, the terms "TV mode" and "tabletop mode/handheld mode" are used below instead of "console mode" and "handheld mode."

TV Mode

In the TV mode type of play, you enjoy gameplay at home by projecting the application screen onto the television.
Because the Switch is in the Switch dock to export videos to the television, this mode assumes that players will play with the Joy-Con controllers detached.
For this reason, in TV mode, system specifications disable any input from Joy-Con controllers that are connected to the console rail.
The user plays using wireless controllers (or controllers that have a USB connection with the Switch dock).

Tabletop Mode

If you remove the Switch from the Switch dock while playing in TV mode, screen output for the application automatically switches from the television to the console screen.
Gameplay in this state, with the system placed on a floor or table and using controllers that are connected wirelessly, is called tabletop mode.

Compared to TV mode, performance and screen size differ (compared to the size of typical TV screens available on the market). However, this play mode is defined as a mode that enables play as you would experience it with a TV, in locations where no TV is present, and also allows use of the touch panel.
Unlike simple handheld systems, by using the console screen and connecting controllers wirelessly, even when outdoors, players can engage in play that takes advantage of the accelerometer and gyroscope of the Joy-Con, and also play in two-player mode.
When tabletop mode is used, in addition to removing the Joy-Con controllers equipped with the console and connecting them wirelessly, it is also possible to connect Pro Controllers or Joy-Con controllers belonging to friends, so the Joy-Con attached to the console may not necessarily be removed. Therefore, take note of this when connecting and using controllers.

Handheld Mode

Handheld mode is when you play while holding the Switch, with the Joy-Con connected to the rails, without using wireless controllers, and as you would for earlier handheld systems.
This mode enables you to play Switch games in locations where there is not enough space to play in tabletop mode, such as on a train or bus.
Handheld mode presumes gameplay involving one device per person.
In addition, playing a game using the touch panel only, without using the Joy-Con connected to the rails or any wireless controller, is also referred to as handheld mode.

Play Modes

As described, a major characteristic of Switch is that users can play anywhere in their preferred modes.

This characteristic of being able to play in a preferred mode would be greatly enjoyed not only by users who want to play games at any time and anywhere, but also users who only play at home by projecting on a TV screen, or those who only play on a commuter train.

For this reason, we want every application to operate in every play mode so that the user can freely choose where they play.
