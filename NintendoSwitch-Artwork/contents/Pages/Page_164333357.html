2 Rules for Using Artwork

Although the artwork included in this package can be used as is for purposes such as Nintendo Switch applications and related printed materials, the composition of colors and lines in this artwork can also be tailored to fit the brand concept and look of each game.

The following are examples of such alterations.

  • Changing to more appropriate colors, or adding patterns
  • Adding shadows, glow effects, textures, or borders as needed
  • Simplifying the artwork by using just its outline, or removing the outline entirely
  • Changing to a more digital or hand-drawn feel

However, be careful to not arrange them in ways that could be misinterpreted by the users.

The following are examples of arrangements that could confuse users.

  • The artwork is arranged in a way that causes the user to misjudge the size of the product.
    Example: The Joy-Con controllers appear larger than the Nintendo Switch Pro Controller when they are both shown together in the same space. (Obviously, the Joy-Con controllers are smaller.)
  • Making a product appear as a different one by removing or changing the elements that are unique to that product, making it hard to understand what the drawing represents.
    One example would be removing the rails from the Nintendo Switch console and the Joy-Con controllers to make it resemble a Wii U GamePad.

Illustrations are available in both the RGB and CMYK color modes. Use the RGB version when working with applications or websites. Use the CMYK version when working with printed materials.

Note:

If anything about using or arranging the illustrations is unclear, or if you want to use a particular effect, contact your Nintendo representative.
