0133: Handling afterimages and flicker

Your application must not allow afterimages or flicker to develop on the LCD screen of the system as a result of repeated changes in hue at the exact same interval.
Software to Be Tested

Applications that display images on the LCD screen of the system
Backgrounder

Generally speaking, if the exact same change in RGB values is cycled to the same pixel of an LCD screen over and over again within a short period of time, the screen tends to develop flickering or afterimages resembling screen burn-in. If your application repeatedly displays a change in hue over and over in a short period of time, you must implement this display effect in a way that avoids causing afterimages or flickering.

The following image, where a white bar alternates between horizontal and vertical once per frame, is an example of a repeated short-term change in hue that is likely to cause afterimages or flickering. (Click to display the image.)

Example of a white bar alternating every frame between horizontal and vertical
Figure 1 Example of a white bar alternating every frame between horizontal and vertical

In a display effect like this, the pixels in the pink sections of the figure showing "Figure 2 Locations where flicker develops" alternate between white and black every frame. This repeated alternation back and forth causes flickering to develop in these pixels.

Figure 2 Locations where flicker develops

Alternating between two hues on every odd frame, as in the above example, is likely to trigger afterimages and flickering. Looping an animation whose length is an even number of frames is also likely to cause afterimages or flickering. See the References for details.

Test Method

Check the source code.
Pass/Fail Determination

Passes if the application does not cause afterimages or flicker to develop on the LCD screen of the system.
Tips

The conditions that cause afterimages and flicker to develop are different for different applications, because they depend on the application's design. This means it is not possible to provide a common Test Method that works for all applications, but for your reference here is one possible example of how to check for afterimages or flickering.

  1. Play the application in either handheld mode or tabletop mode, concentrating on the scenes or locations that show indications of flickering.
  2. After playing, return to the HOME Menu, confirm that the theme is set to basic black, and check whether flickering is still visible.

Setting the background to a dark color while the LCD screen is receiving power makes it easier to see whether afterimages or flicker have developed. (It is not possible to see flicker when displaying a completely black image.)

Accelerometer

This subchapter covers requirements that apply when using the accelerometer.

0006: Taking at-rest noise into account
If you use accelerometer output values, take into account the possibility that these values include up to ±0.02 G of at-rest noise, and ensure that this amount of error in the output values does not prevent users from progressing in your application.
Software to Be Tested
Applications that use the accelerometer.
Backgrounder

Accelerometer output values can be inaccurate by up to ±0.02 G due to noise in the sensor itself, called at-rest noise. Due to individual hardware differences between sensors, some sensors may continually report values containing up to ±0.02 G of error. This means that if your application must detect when the sensor is at rest, but you assume that even small changes in values indicate motion, then your application may never successfully detect that the sensor is "at rest" no matter how long the user waits. This in turn could prevent the user from progressing in your application.

To ensure that the user is able to progress, implement your application to accommodate error of up to ±0.02 G in the accelerometer output values.

Info:

Aside from at-rest noise, the operation of other devices such as the vibration motor and fans may also affect the accelerometer output values.

Passes if the player can progress in your application without issues in scenes where the accelerometer is used, even in the presence of at-rest noise.

0042: Taking zero-point drift into account

Your application must take into account the possibility of up to ±0.13 G of error in the accelerometer values it takes as input, and ensure that this amount of error in the values does not prevent users from progressing in your application.
Software to Be Tested
Applications that use the accelerometer.
Backgrounder

The zero-point of the accelerometer may accumulate a drift of up to ±0.13 G over time.

For this reason, you must ensure that your application can handle even a zero-point drift of ±0.13 G without problems when determining that the accelerometer is at rest, or when using very small changes in acceleration. The following are some ways of handling this.

  • Avoid implementations that require acceleration to reach a specific threshold value before the user can progress to the next scene.
  • Set a timeout when determining whether the accelerometer is at rest. This prevents your application from getting blocked by the at-rest determination.
  • When you use very small changes in acceleration, take into account the possibility that 0.13 G of drift may occur.

If your application calculates relative angles based on accelerometer values, note that this accelerometer error causes drift in the calculated angles as well.

For example, in contrast to the theoretical gravitational acceleration (x,y,z) = (0,0,1), an accelerometer experiencing ±0.13 G of error could yield the values (x,y,z) = (0.13,0.13,0.87). Under these conditions the angles calculated by your application would drift by roughly 12°.

You must design your application to accommodate this potential level of drift.

Test Method

Note: Perform this test with all accelerometer-equipped controllers that the application supports.

  1. Launch the DevMenu. Under Controller, configure the following settings for each LED pattern required for your test.
    1. For Accelerometer, set Resting Point to Min.
    2. For Accelerometer, set Sensitivity to 0.
  2. Reconnect the controllers.
  3. Press the HOME Button to exit the DevMenu, then start the application.
  4. Play through, from beginning to end, all scenes that use the accelerometer.
  5. Launch the DevMenu again. Under Controller, configure the following settings for each LED pattern required for your test.
    1. For Accelerometer, set Resting Point to Max.
    2. For Accelerometer, set Sensitivity to 0.
  6. Reconnect the controllers.
  7. Press the HOME Button to exit the DevMenu, then start the application.
  8. Play through, from beginning to end, all scenes that use the accelerometer.
Pass/Fail Determination

Passes if, in steps 4 and 8, the user can progress in the application without problems.
Tips

  • The settings you configured go into effect after you select Apply Changes and reconnect the controllers.
  • Restarting the system returns these settings to their default values.

0010: Taking at-rest noise into account
If you use gyroscope output values, take into account the possibility that these values include up to ±1.8 dps of at-rest noise, and ensure that this amount of error in the output values does not prevent users from progressing in your application.
Software to Be Tested
Applications that use the gyroscope.
Backgrounder

The angular velocity output values yielded by the gyroscope can be inaccurate by up to ±1.8 dps due to noise in the sensor itself, called at-rest noise. This means that if your application must detect small changes in angular velocity in order for the user to progress, then the error of up to ±1.8 dps in the gyroscope output values could prevent your application from successfully detecting those small changes. This in turn could prevent the user from progressing in your application.

To ensure that the user is able to progress, implement your application to accommodate error of up to ±1.8 dps in the gyroscope output values.

Info:

Aside from at-rest noise, the operation of other devices such as the vibration motor and fans may also affect the gyroscope output values.

Also note that the gyroscope at-rest noise may increase while the IR Motion Camera is in use.

0044: Taking sensitivity drift into account

Your application must accommodate error of up to ±8% in both the angular velocity measured by the gyroscope and the angles calculated from that angular velocity, and ensure that this amount of error in the values does not prevent users from progressing in your application.
Software to Be Tested
Applications that use the gyroscope.
Backgrounder

Due to variation between individual gyroscopes, deterioration of the gyroscope, or changes in temperature or humidity, the values that the gyroscope provides may contain a margin of error of up to ±8%. You must design your application to accommodate this error.

Likewise, the fact that the angular velocity contains error means that the angles (the orientation in space) you calculate from the angular velocity also contain up to ±8% error. For example, if the angular velocity the gyroscope obtains is off by +8%, the value the gyroscope obtains when rotated 90° would become (90° × 1.08) = 97.2°. This drift in angle is cumulative for continued use of the gyroscope. You must design your application in such a way that it operates normally even with this level of drift.

Attaching and detaching devices

This subchapter covers requirements that apply when your application detects that a device has been attached or detached.

0050: Prohibition of specifications that give the user reason to dock/undock the system
Your application must not give the user reasons to increase the number of times the system has been docked and undocked.
Software to Be Tested
All applications
Backgrounder

The system has an electrical connection to the dock. Repeatedly docking and undocking the system causes wear and tear on these contacts. Because of these constraints on their mechanical durability, your application must not incorporate any gameplay element that gives the user reason to increase the number of times the system has been docked and undocked.

Unacceptable examples:
  • Resetting damage or fatigue accumulated by the player character every time the system is docked
  • Treating the act of docking or undocking the system as a gameplay operation and asking the user to perform it repeatedly
Acceptable examples:
  • Suggesting that the user dock the system and use the TV screen to play because you recommend playing a specific minigame in TV mode
  • Suggesting that the user dock the system and use the TV screen to play because the system's remaining battery life is getting low
  • Playing a sound, auto-saving, or pausing when the system is docked or undocked
  • Displaying a message prompting the user to undock the system in a scene that uses the touch screen

The situations listed above are only a few examples. This is not a comprehensive list of all applicable cases.
Test Method
Play through the application from beginning to end.
Pass/Fail Determination

Passes if all of the following conditions are met.

  • The application does not frequently display messages or play audio prompting the user to dock or undock the system.
  • The application does not tie an incentive to docking or undocking the system that would give the user reason to repeatedly dock or undock the system.

0094: Prohibition of specifications that give the user reason to attach/detach the Joy-Con controllers
Your application must not give the user reasons to increase the number of times the Joy-Con controllers have been attached and detached from the system.
Software to Be Tested
All applications
Backgrounder

The Joy-Con controllers have an electrical connection to the system. Repeatedly attaching and detaching them causes wear and tear on these contacts. Because of these constraints on their mechanical durability, your application must not incorporate any gameplay element that gives the user reason to increase the number of times the Joy-Con controllers have been attached and detached.

Unacceptable examples:
  • Treating the act of attaching or detaching the Joy-Con controllers as a gameplay operation and asking the user to perform it repeatedly
  • Using the act of detaching and reattaching a Joy-Con as a "reload ammunition" operation in your application
Acceptable examples:
  • Playing a sound, auto-saving, or pausing when the user attaches or detaches the Joy-Con controllers
  • Displaying a message instructing users how to hold the Joy-Con controllers after detaching them from the system

The situations listed above are only a few examples. This is not a comprehensive list of all applicable cases.

Note that this guideline is not intended to restrict the messaging or instructions you use to show the user how to hold the Joy-Con controllers when playing your game.
Test Method
Play through the application from beginning to end.
Pass/Fail Determination

Passes if all of the following conditions are met.

  • The application does not frequently display messages or play audio prompting the user to attach or detach the Joy-Con controllers.
  • The application does not tie an incentive to attaching or detaching the Joy-Con controllers that would give the user reason to repeatedly attach or detach the Joy-Con controllers from the system.

Microphone

This subchapter covers requirements that apply when using a microphone.

0138: Supporting the Nintendo USB microphone and Nintendo USB wireless microphone
Your application must support both the Nintendo USB microphone and wireless microphone.
Software to Be Tested

Applications that use USB microphones
Exceptions
Applications that use audio input features only for voice chat
Backgrounder

Applications that use a USB microphone must support both the Nintendo USB microphone and the Nintendo USB wireless microphone. However, the Nintendo USB wireless microphone is sold exclusively in Japan. If Japan is not part of the regions in which you are releasing your application, you do not need to support the Nintendo USB wireless microphone.

Info:

The Nintendo USB microphone and Nintendo USB wireless microphone have the same specifications as the Wii U microphone and Wii U wireless microphone respectively.
That means that if your application supports the Wii U microphone and Wii U wireless microphone, it also supports the Nintendo USB microphone and Nintendo USB wireless microphone.
References
  • NintendoSDK Documents
    • Features > Audio > AUDIO Library > Manual > Features
Test Method
If Japan is part of the regions in which you are releasing your application:

Go to a scene in the application that uses a USB microphone, and use both the Nintendo USB microphone and the Nintendo USB wireless microphone.

If Japan is not part of the regions in which you are releasing your application:

Go to a scene in the application that uses a USB microphone, and use the Nintendo USB microphone.
Pass/Fail Determination
If Japan is part of the regions in which you are releasing your application:

Passes if both the Nintendo USB microphone and the Nintendo USB wireless microphone function without problems.

If Japan is not part of the regions in which you are releasing your application:

Passes if the Nintendo USB microphone functions without problems.

0139: Implementing in a way that does not depend on specific characteristics of any supported microphone
Your application's functionality must not depend on the characteristics of any specific microphone it supports.
Software to Be Tested
Applications that use microphones
Backgrounder

The characteristics of microphones differ depending on the model, and there may be variation between microphones of the same model. When you are coding your application, you must ensure that it is not dependent on specific characteristics of any of the microphones it supports.

Examples of unacceptable implementations:

  • Application does not allow the user to proceed unless it receives high-pitched input that cannot be detected by the Nintendo USB wireless microphone
Info:

For details on the specific characteristics of the Nintendo USB microphone and Nintendo USB wireless microphone, see NintendoSDK Documents > Features > Audio > audio Library > Manual > Features > Audio Input > Information About Using Microphone Input Features.
References
  • NintendoSDK Documents
    • Features > Audio > audio Library > Manual > Features > Audio Input > Information About Using Microphone Input Features
      • Common to All Platforms > Notes About the Characteristics of the Microphone
Test Method

In a scene that uses a microphone, use all types of microphones listed below that the application supports.

  • Nintendo USB microphone
  • Nintendo USB wireless microphone (see Note 1)
  • Headset (see Note 2)

Note 1: If the Nintendo USB wireless microphone is not available for sale in the regions where you are releasing your application, use any Nintendo-licensed USB wireless microphone. If no Nintendo-licensed USB wireless microphones are available for sale, you are not required to perform this test with a USB wireless microphone.

Note 2: Nintendo recommends using any commercially-available headset with a boom microphone.
Pass/Fail Determination

Passes if the application's behavior does not depend on the specific characteristics of any supported microphone.
