2.1 Joy-Con and Play Modes

Joy-Con

Figure 2-1 Joy-Con

The Joy-Con™ is a pair of left and right independent wireless controllers. As shown in Figure 2-2 Ways to Hold the Joy-Con, there are several different ways to hold the Joy-Con, providing a variety of possible styles of play.

Because the two Joy-Con controllers have the same combined number of buttons as the Wii U GamePad, the pair can be held and used like a full-fledged multi-button controller for rich, solo, gameplay. The Joy-Con left and right controllers can be combined and used as a single controller by attaching them to the Joy-Con grip. You can play together with another person when you provide that person with one of the Joy-Con controllers. You no longer need to have additional controllers available. You can also hold a single Joy-Con controller horizontally with a button configuration similar to the Super Nintendo Entertainment System controller. Both the left and right Joy-Con controllers include an accelerometer and gyroscope sensor, allowing physical gameplay through hand and arm motions. A player holding both controllers can move the left and right Joy-Con independently and enter different inputs for each. You can also attach the Joy-Con controllers to the Nintendo Switch™ console. By attaching the right and left Joy-Con controllers to the console, Nintendo Switch can be played similarly to a handheld game system.

When the Joy-Con controllers are connected to the console, they communicate through a wired connection instead of wirelessly.

Figure 2-2 Ways to Hold the Joy-Con

In addition, the Joy-Con controllers include an IR Motion Camera that can detect the shape of objects, and a linear resonant actuator for a diverse range of vibration types, which are features not seen in previous platforms. For more information about the other features and components built into the Joy-Con, see 3.2 Joy-Con Hardware Configuration.

IR Motion Camera

The IR Motion Camera operates using IR LEDs and an IR camera. This component is only included in the Joy-Con (R) controller.

Objects illuminated with the IR LEDs are captured with the IR camera to obtain an IR image. The hardware processes these IR images to recognize the movement and shape of objects. Because it uses infrared, this feature functions in dark locations as well. This enables the following features.

  • Recognition of quick hand movements and hand shapes
    • Recognizes the rotation, shaking, pitch, and position of hands, in addition to the shape, number, and outline of fingers.
  • Optical attachments
    • Attachments made only of plastic with no electric parts can be created if materials such as recursive retroreflectors are used.

For more information, see the Nintendo Switch IR Motion Camera Guide.

HD Rumble

HD rumble is a high-performance vibration feature that makes use of a linear resonant actuator. The linear resonant actuator is fundamentally different from the eccentric rotating mass installed in the Wii U GamePad and other devices. This component is built into both the right and left Joy-Con controllers.

HD rumble has keener responsiveness than the conventional vibration feature, and by changing the intensity and frequency of the vibrations you can express a richer variety of sensations. Beyond simple changes in vibration strength, this provides a way to realistically express the feel of physical objects, like the rolling of a lightweight ball or the flowing of water. With HD rumble, vibrations can be used in combination with images and sounds in game scenes where vibrations would not have been used in the past.

To use the vibration feature in an application, you can create vibrations in each scene in the conventional way, or you can play vibration data that was created in advance. Vibration data can be created using a tool included in the development environment which can, for example, convert sound data to vibrations.

Taking full advantage of the richly expressive vibrations of HD rumble enables applications to offer more immersive experiences than ever before.

For more information, see the Nintendo Switch HD Rumble Guide.

Play Modes

Nintendo Switch can change play modes depending on whether the system is sitting in the Switch dock and whether the Joy-Con controllers are attached or detached. In this regard, Switch is a "console to go."

The various play modes are described below. For more information about each play mode and how to support them in your application, see Nintendo Switch Play Mode Guide.

TV Mode

Figure 2-3 Nintendo Switch in TV Mode

When set in the Switch dock, Switch can be played as a regular game console, using the TV as the display screen while using the Joy-Con controllers to play the game.

Tabletop Mode

Figure 2-4 Nintendo Switch in Tabletop Mode


If you remove the Switch console from the Switch dock while the system is in TV mode, the images that were being shown on the TV are now shown on the Switch screen. The Switch console is equipped with a kickstand that can be used to prop the console on a table or other surface for play in tabletop mode, where you play using the detached Joy-Con while looking at the Switch screen. Thanks to Nintendo Switch, the experience of a game console, where groups of people play with controllers while watching a game on the same, single screen, is untethered from the TV and made portable.

When the Switch console is removed from the Switch dock and moved around, it behaves differently in numerous ways. For more information, see Differences in Behavior When Removed From the Switch Dock.

If you connect a separate Joy-Con to Switch Lite, it can also be played in tabletop mode.

Handheld Mode

Figure 2-5 Switch in Handheld Mode

By attaching the Joy-Con controllers to the console, Nintendo Switch can be played similarly to a handheld game system. When the Joy-Con is attached in this manner, communication between the Joy-Con and the Switch console takes place over a wired connection.
